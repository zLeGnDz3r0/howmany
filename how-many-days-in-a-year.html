    How many days in a year?

    A common Gregorian year has 365 days. A leap year has 366 (29 February). USNO rule: years divisible by 4, except century years not divisible by 400. 1900 was not a leap year; 2000 was; 2100 will not be. This civil year is not a sidereal year.

    Using a common Gregorian year: 365 days. Leap years add 29 February.

    Leap-year rule

    USNO: years evenly divisible by 4 are leap years, except century years that are not evenly divisible by 400. So 1700, 1800, 1900, and 2100 are not leap years; 1600, 2000, and 2400 are. That puts 97 leap days in 400 years and a mean year of 365.2425 days.

    Gotchas

    • Century years are the trap. 2000 was a leap year (divisible by 400). 1900 was not. 2100 will not be. “Every four years” is the Julian rule, not the Gregorian one.
    • Not a sidereal year. The civil calendar tracks the tropical (seasonal) year. A sidereal year is the orbit relative to the stars (JPL: 365.25636 days) — longer, and not how we number 365/366.
    • Weeks don’t come out even. 365 ÷ 7 leaves 1 day; 366 ÷ 7 leaves 2. See weeks in a year.

    FAQ

    How many days are in a year?

    365 days in a common Gregorian year; 366 in a leap year (29 February). The civil calendar uses this count, not a sidereal year.

    What is the Gregorian leap-year rule?

    USNO: years evenly divisible by 4 are leap years, except century years that are not evenly divisible by 400. 1700, 1800, 1900, and 2100 are not leap years; 1600, 2000, and 2400 are.

    Is a calendar year the same as a sidereal year?

    No. The Gregorian year tracks the tropical (seasonal) year. A sidereal year is Earth’s orbit relative to the stars — a different astronomical year (JPL: 365.25636 days).

    How long is the average Gregorian year?

    365.2425 days: 97 leap days every 400 years (USNO). That is close to the tropical year (about 365.2422 days), not the sidereal year.
