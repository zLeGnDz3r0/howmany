    How many weeks in a year?

    A common year is 52 weeks + 1 day: 365 ÷ 7 = 52 remainder 1. A leap year is 52 weeks + 2 days: 366 ÷ 7 = 52 remainder 2. The leftover days are why a calendar year is not a whole number of weeks. ISO week years are a different count (52 or 53 full weeks).

    Using a common year: 365 ÷ 7 = 52 weeks remainder 1 day.

    ISO weeks are not leftover days

    ISO 8601 numbers whole Monday–Sunday weeks. Week 1 is the week that includes the year’s first Thursday. A week-numbering year then has 52 or 53 calendar weeks — not “52 + 1 day.” Days next to 1 January can belong to week 52/53 of the previous ISO year.

    Gotchas

    • Don’t quote 52.1429 without the division. 365 ÷ 7 = 52 + 1/7 = 52.142857… (repeating). Truncating to 52.14 or 52.1429 hides the remainder of 1 day.
    • ISO weeks ≠ calendar leftover. Payroll and “week 53” talk uses ISO 8601 (52 or 53 full weeks), not 365 ÷ 7.
    • Leap years add a second leftover day, not an extra week. 366 ÷ 7 still has remainder 2. Leap-day rule: days in a year.

    FAQ

    How many weeks are in a year?

    52 weeks plus leftover days. 365 ÷ 7 = 52 remainder 1 (common year). 366 ÷ 7 = 52 remainder 2 (leap year).

    Is it 52.1429 weeks?

    365 ÷ 7 = 52 + 1/7, which is the repeating decimal 52.142857… . Rounding that to 52.1429 hides the remainder. This page shows the division.

    Do ISO weeks match the calendar year?

    Not always. ISO 8601 week 1 is the week that includes the year’s first Thursday. An ISO week-numbering year has 52 or 53 full Monday–Sunday weeks.

    How many weeks in a leap year?

    Still 52 weeks plus leftover days: 366 ÷ 7 = 52 remainder 2.
