    Fluid ounces vs ounces

    A fluid ounce is volume (how much space). An ounce (avoirdupois) is weight (how much mass). Saying “8 ounces in a cup” is true for US fluid ounces. It is false for flour on a scale.

    Flour, sugar, butter, brown sugar, powdered sugar: King Arthur Baking Ingredient Weight Chart. Water: 1 mL ≈ 1 g near 4 °C; US cup = 236.588 mL. oz weight = grams ÷ 28.349523125.

    Gotchas

    • Water is the coincidence. 1 mL of water ≈ 1 g near 4 °C, so 1 US cup ≈ 236.588 g ≈ 8.345 oz by weight — close to 8, not equal, and still a different unit.
    • Flour is the trap. 1 US cup of all-purpose flour is 8 fl oz of volume and 120 g (King Arthur), about 4¼ oz by weight.
    • Don’t multiply fl oz × 28.3495 to get grams of a liquid unless you mean “this stuff actually weighs one ounce per fluid ounce,” which most baking ingredients do not.

    FAQ

    Are fluid ounces the same as ounces?

    No. Fluid ounces measure volume. Ounces (avoirdupois) measure weight.

    So how many ounces are in a cup?

    8 US fluid ounces of volume. Weight ounces depend on the ingredient: water about 8.345 oz, all-purpose flour 120 g (about 4¼ oz) per King Arthur.

    Why is water close to 8 oz?

    Because 1 mL of water is about 1 g, and a US cup is 236.588 mL, so about 236.588 g ≈ 8.345 oz by weight. The units are still different.
