    How many BTU to cool a room?

    Sizing estimate, not an HVAC design. ENERGY STAR maps floor area to room-air-conditioner capacity (8-foot ceiling). Sun, shade, extra people, and kitchens have published adjustments. Climate and tall ceilings are caveats — ENERGY STAR does not give multipliers for those here, so we do not invent them. This search spikes in summer and is near-zero in winter.

    Kitchen?

    ENERGY STAR: if more than two people regularly occupy the room, add 600 BTU for each additional person. We do not subtract for one occupant — they did not publish that.

    Gotchas

    • Estimate, not Manual J. ACCA Manual J is the residential load-calculation standard (insulation, windows, orientation, climate). A room-AC shopping chart is not that.
    • 8-foot ceiling. ENERGY STAR says capacities assume an 8-ft ceiling and, for higher ceilings, to consider more capacity. They do not publish a multiplier, so we don’t invent one.
    • Too big is worse. ENERGY STAR: an oversized unit cools before it dehumidifies. Match the chart; don’t “round way up.”
    • Summer query, winter silence. Cooling BTU is a summer spike. Heating is a different load. This chart is room air conditioners, not a whole-house furnace.

    Sources

    ENERGY STAR, Room Air Conditioners: square-footage capacity chart (8-ft ceiling); shade −10%; sun +10%; +600 BTU per person above two; +4,000 BTU in a kitchen. energystar.gov/products/room_air_conditioners. 1 ton = 12,000 Btu/h: U.S. DOE Uniform Methods Project Ch. 4; DOE HVAC Right-Sizing webinar. ACCA Manual J is cited as the proper design method, not used numerically here.

    FAQ

    How many BTU do I need to cool a room?

    Use the ENERGY STAR room air-conditioner chart by floor area. This is a sizing estimate, not an ACCA Manual J design.

    Is that 20 BTU per square foot?

    A flat 20 BTU/sq ft is a common sales starting point. This page uses ENERGY STAR’s capacity chart, not a flat 20.

    How many tons is that?

    1 ton of cooling = 12,000 Btu/h (U.S. DOE). Divide BTU/h by 12,000.

    Why does this search spike in summer?

    Room-AC sizing is a hot-weather question. In winter the same search is near zero.
