    How many ounces in a cup of flour?

    1 US cup of all-purpose flour = 120 grams = about 4¼ ounces by weight (King Arthur Baking). That is not 8 ounces. A US cup is always 8 fluid ounces of volume; flour does not weigh 8 oz in that cup.

    Method (assumption)

    King Arthur: “For best results, we recommend weighing your ingredients with a digital scale. A cup of all-purpose flour weighs 120 grams or 4¼ ounces.” Brown sugar on their chart is packed; this flour figure is not a packed-cup number. We do not add an unsourced scoop-vs-spoon toggle.

    Gotchas

    • 8 fl oz ≠ 8 oz of flour. The cup is 8 fluid ounces of space. The flour in it weighs 120 g, about 4.23 oz (King Arthur lists 4¼ oz). Fluid ounces vs ounces.
    • This is King Arthur’s cup, their method. They recommend a digital scale. Their chart is the published 120 g / 4¼ oz per US cup of all-purpose flour. This site does not invent a “scooped” or “sifted” extra number.
    • Other flours differ. Only all-purpose flour at 120 g/cup is on this page. Bread flour is also 120 g on the same chart; whole-wheat is not. Use the ingredient converter for sugar, butter, cocoa, and the rest we sourced.

    FAQ

    How many ounces are in a cup of flour?

    About 4¼ ounces by weight (120 grams) for all-purpose flour, per King Arthur — not 8 ounces. The cup is still 8 fluid ounces of volume.

    Is that the same as 8 ounces in a cup?

    No. 8 ounces in a cup is 8 US fluid ounces (volume). Flour in that cup weighs 120 g.

    Whose gram number is this?

    King Arthur Baking Ingredient Weight Chart: all-purpose flour, 1 cup = 120 g = 4¼ oz. They recommend weighing.
