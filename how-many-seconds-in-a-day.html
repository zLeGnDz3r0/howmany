    How many seconds in a day?

    A common day is counted as 86,400 SI seconds: 24 × 60 × 60. The SI second is BIPM’s caesium-133 definition, not “whatever Earth did today.” UTC can insert a leap second (IERS); those uncommon UTC days have 86,401 seconds. This page’s default is the common 86,400-second day.

    Fractions work: 1/2, 1/24. Result is 86,400 SI seconds per common day. Leap seconds are not added here.

    Gotchas

    • 86,400 is the count, not Earth’s exact spin. BIPM defines the second independently of the rotating Earth. A mean solar day is no longer exactly 86,400 SI seconds; that is why leap seconds exist.
    • Leap seconds are infrequent and announced. IERS decides; BIPM publishes UTC. IERS: |UT1 − UTC| is kept under 0.9 s. A positive leap second makes that UTC day 86,401 s. We do not claim every year gets one, or that negative leap seconds have happened (IERS: none so far in the series they describe).
    • Don’t bake leap seconds into 365 × 86,400. A common year of 365 common days is 31,536,000 s. Extra UTC leap seconds, if any, sit outside that product. See days in a year.

    FAQ

    How many seconds are in a day?

    86,400 SI seconds in a common day: 24 hours × 60 minutes × 60 seconds. That is the usual civil count.

    Is the second still 1/86,400 of a day?

    Not as the SI definition. BIPM: the second is defined by the caesium-133 hyperfine frequency (9,192,631,770 Hz). Historically the second was 1/86,400 of a mean solar day; Earth’s rotation is not that uniform.

    What about leap seconds?

    IERS may insert a leap second in UTC so |UT1 − UTC| stays under 0.9 s. BIPM computes TAI/UTC. A UTC day with a positive leap second has 86,401 SI seconds. That is occasional, announced in IERS Bulletin C — not every day.

    How many seconds in a week or a year?

    Using 86,400 s/day: 7 days = 604,800 s; a common year 365 × 86,400 = 31,536,000 s; a leap year 366 × 86,400 = 31,622,400 s. Leap seconds are extra and are not in those products.
