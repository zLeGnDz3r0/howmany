    How many gallons of paint?

    Room length × width × wall height → wall area. Behr publishes 250–400 sq ft per gallon per coat. We do not invent a 350 midpoint. Choose 400 (smooth, primed) or 250 (porous, textured). Doors and windows use Behr’s labeled averages (20 and 15 sq ft). Second coat is optional and labeled.

    Gotchas

    • 250 and 400 are both Behr’s numbers. Porous, rough, or a dramatic color change uses more paint (closer to 250). Smooth primed walls can reach 400. We don’t average them into a fake 350.
    • Door and window sizes are averages. Behr: ~15 sq ft per window, ~20 sq ft per door. Measure yours if they are unusual.
    • Second coat is a choice. Default here is two coats because color changes usually need it — labeled, and you can set one.
    • Spray waste is not in the coverage. Behr TDS: figures exclude spraying loss.

    FAQ

    How many gallons of paint do I need?

    Wall area ÷ coverage per coat, times coats. Behr lists 250–400 sq ft per gallon. Round up.

    How do I measure the walls?

    Perimeter × height. Rectangle: 2 × (length + width) × height. Subtract openings.

    Do I need two coats?

    Often yes. Behr says to factor in a second coat if necessary. That choice is labeled here.

    Does spraying change the number?

    Behr’s TDS says coverage does not include spray loss.
